## R1 - Game research
Game: Half life
-The goal of Half-Life is to survive the disaster at the Black Mesa research facility and escape while fighting alien creatures and hostile soldiers. The player controls Gordon Freeman, solving puzzles, exploring the facility, and overcoming obstacles to progress through the story 

-Half-Life is a 1998 first-person shooter developed by Valve. It influenced many later games by combining storytelling, combat, puzzles, and realistic-feeling environments. Its engine, GoldSrc, was based on a modified version of Quake's engine and was used for games like Counter-Strike and Day of Defeat. GoldSrc helped developers create new games using existing technology, but modern engines like Unity and Unreal offer more advanced graphics and easier tools for many types of games.

-- Developer: Valve.
- Release: November 1998 for PC.
- Genre: First-person shooter (FPS), science fiction, and action.
- Main character: Gordon Freeman, a theoretical physicist working at the Black Mesa Research Facility.
- Original technology: A heavily modified version of the Quake engine, known as the GoldSrc engine.
The story begins when a scientific experiment goes wrong at Black Mesa, a secret research facility. The accident opens a connection to another dimension, allowing hostile alien creatures to enter the facility. Gordon Freeman must survive, fight enemies, solve environmental puzzles, and navigate the consequences of the disaster.

One reason Half-Life stood out was its storytelling. Rather than frequently stopping the game for separate cinematic scenes, it often allowed the player to experience events directly through Gordon's perspective while still controlling the character. This helped make the story feel like something the player was experiencing instead of simply watching.

## R2 - Three engines and my choice
1.Godot: Supports 2D and 3D games and uses GDScript or C#. A useful feature is its free, open-source editor. Games made with Godot include Brotato, Cassette Beasts, and Dome Keeper. Official site: https://godotengine.org/

2.Unity: Supports 2D and 3D games and uses C#. A useful feature is its Asset Store, which provides tools and resources for developers. Games made with Unity include Among Us, Cuphead, and Hollow Knight. Official site: https://unity.com/

3.Stride: Supports 3D game development and uses C#. A useful feature is its integration with the .NET ecosystem. It is used for C# game development, although it has fewer widely recognized commercial game examples than Unity or Godot. Official site: https://www.stride3d.net/

-Reason: I would choose Unity because I have some experience with C#, and its tools, tutorials, and large community could help me create my own games.
